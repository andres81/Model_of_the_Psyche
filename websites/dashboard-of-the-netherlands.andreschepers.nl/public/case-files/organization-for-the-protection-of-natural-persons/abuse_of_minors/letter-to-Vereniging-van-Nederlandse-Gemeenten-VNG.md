Dear municipalities of The Netherlands,

I wrote one of you, the municipality of Harlingen, with regard to Tara: She died
while, in my opinion, that could have been prevented. Warning after warning from
the Onderzoeksraad voor Veiligheid, accident after accident, and yet, she was
allowed on that ship, that killed her. She trusted us, society, with her life
that we would keep her safe. We failed.

The question now is to what point you are responsible for the safety in
situations that happen in your municipality. What duty of care does a mayor
have? If you know a situation is unsafe, potentially lethal like the bruine
vloot ships, don't you have a duty of care, a moral obligation, as a mayor, but
also as a human being, to do everything in your power to prevent people from
entering such a situation?

What did you do as an organization after this incident? This terrible accident?
Did you discuss ways to prevent this from ever happening again? Did you go over
how this could happen while it was very well known that those ships were
dangerous? If not, why not? Don't you wish to prevent this from happening again,
especially if you look at the fact that this was a known risk? If you discussed
this, what was the outcome? How can I check to what extent I can trust the moral
systems that municipalities and mayors of this country are? What have you done
in your municipalities to prevent unnecessary accidents like these?

If you did not discuss this, are you then saying that it is not your
responsibility? Is that known by high schools, for example, that might
implicitly trust you that if one is capable of using ships of a bruine vloot for
example, that it then must be safe? Because they believe that if it was known to
be unsafe, like the bruine vloot clearly was according to the Onderzoeksraad
voor Veiligheid, it must have been shut down by the municipality or the ILT. So
what is your view of the contract a municipality in The Netherlands has with
society? Did policies change after the accident Tara died in?

The question is now: How safe are children in our society if everybody neglects
to take their responsibility? Aren't we doomed to freedom, according to Sartre
and de Beauvoir, who both are responsible for L'Être et le Néant?

I demand a description of explicit contracts between municipalities and society
that both parties explicitly agreed upon regarding responsibilities and duties
of care towards each other. You, municipalities of The Netherlands have lost my
trust: Eindhoven water cannon incident, Tara, femicide, etc. We go for explicit
contracts that you can be held to, and otherwise societal escalations. These
escalations, just to be sure you understand how far I go, are up to the level of
international institutions. That means the European Court for Human Rights for
example. I have just gotten started!

André Schepers
