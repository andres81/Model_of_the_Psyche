21 October 2026, The Netherlands

Courthouses and courts of appeal,

I write to you in regard to the torture of children in Spain. Apparently, the
Dutch childcare benefits scandal is not enough for The Netherlands. I would like
to address two aspects of this situation:

* To what extent were judges involved in ruling that children should be sent
  to Spain and were the judges aware of torture?
* The conviction of a father that knew something was off and took his child
  back from Spain back to The Netherlands.

Let it be very clear: We are talking about torture, not 'just' abuse of
children. After Dachau during Nazi Germany and other concentration camps, we
made one thing very clear at the European level: Never again torture. And
with the European Court of Human Rights it was enforced that nations abide
by this agreement or prosecution will follow. If you did not do something
while you knew, aren't you then also in part responsible for child
torture? And because you act on behalf of the state, The Netherlands, can
your passive behavior be a reason to drag The Netherlands in front of this
court? With this letter I want to ask for answers from all the judges of The
Netherlands in The Netherlands, and inform you that the judges of the court
cases involving the father, that was doing his civil duty by taking his kid
back should never have been convicted: Therefore, I will drag the judges
involved to the market square of The Netherlands and ask in public for a
justification. In this letter I will give a justification for this, the
functional architecture of the legal system and of Dutch society, but most
importantly of a democracy, and how that architecture dictates the necessity
I am subject to. You have put me with my back against the wall. I have to
fight now for justice and for the democracy of The Netherlands, but also
give a signal to the international community that I will not tolerate this,
ever! When a child is tortured, and a father does nót bring his child back,
thát is reprimandable. What has happened constitutes a gross neglect of civil
and moral duty by the judges. Therefore, I will give them an opportunity to
defend their actions. Otherwise, I will ask the international community to
scrutinise the actions of these judges, as well as the legal system in general.

## General knowledge: Situational Awareness

To what extent were judges involved in ruling that children should be sent
to Spain and were the judges aware of torture? If not to what extent were
you sure this was a safe situation to send chidlren to? The inspection
indicated they cannot check situations outside of The Netherlands, so how
could you have been sure it was safe? Do you take your civil duty serious
first and foremost that we will never ever allow torture ever again within
Europe?

## The father that brought his child back to The Netherlands

The conviction of a father that knew something was off and took his child
back from Spain back to The Netherlands. A judge of a courthouse and
judges of a court of appeal bóth convicted this father, but it did not
become clear what these judges did subsequently: Weren't you made aware
due to these court cases that torture was going on? In addition, why was
he convicted without looking at the circumstances? What about the moral of
the law, "the geest van de wet", that, if applied in this case, should
never have resulted in a conviction but instead in a "thank you" to the
gentleman for protecting a child from torture? What became not clear here?
The situation was only stopped by Nieuwsuur and its escalation to the
Spanish authorities. This is like the Dutch childcare benefits scandal
that took way too long for it to be stopped. How can we citizens trust the
rule of law, the legal system, if judges go autopilot?
